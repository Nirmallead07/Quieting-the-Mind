# Quieting the Mind

A short reflection on overthinking, open to everyone: lay people, religious and clergy alike. It looks at how the mind replays the past, worries about the future, doubts decisions, reads other people, and judges itself. It is a companion to [Living My Vows](https://nirmallead07.github.io/living-my-vows/).

Prepared by Fr. Solomon Anthony Nirmalan, CMF.

## About the tool

Everyone answers the same items. The situations give examples from home, work, parish and religious community life together, such as "someone in authority over you, such as a boss, a priest or a superior", so that no one has to choose a group.

**Part One: In daily life.** Twenty everyday situations, four in each of five areas: the past, the future, decisions, other people, and conscience. Each option reflects one kind of response: constructive thinking (3), normal reflection (2), repetitive thinking (1), reassurance-seeking or checking (0), or avoidance (0). Careful preparation, prayer and asking one trusted person for advice count as healthy. The order of options is shuffled on every visit. One further question, about having someone to talk to, is not scored.

**Part Two: In my mind.** Twenty statements about the past month on a Never / Sometimes / Often / Always scale, eight of them worded positively, plus four statements about what overthinking costs (sleep, presence, tension, tiredness) and three yes or no statements that gently check for answers that claim a perfectly still mind.

At the end the person sees two marks for each area, one for daily life and one for the mind, an area to give thanks for and an area of invitation to grow, with Scripture, a question for prayer and small steps. They also see how their overthinking usually shows itself (replaying, seeking certainty or stepping back), and a note of care pointing to a trusted person, counsellor or doctor when the cost is high.

The tool is a mirror for reflection, not a validated measurement instrument or a diagnosis. Results should not be used to compare or evaluate people.

The whole tool is one file, `index.html`. It runs in any phone or computer browser. Answers stay on each person's own device and are never sent anywhere, and a "Clear my answers" button removes everything.
