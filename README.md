# Quieting the Mind

A guided self-check on overthinking: how the mind can keep replaying the past, worrying about the future, doubting decisions, guessing what others think, and judging itself too harshly.

Prepared by Fr. Solomon Anthony Nirmalan, CMF.

## About the tool

Careful thinking is a good thing. We prepare for what matters, pray over our choices, ask for advice and learn from our mistakes. Thinking turns into overthinking when it keeps going round the same ground, when it is driven by worry or distress, or when it begins to cost us sleep, peace and the freedom to act. Quieting the Mind helps a person notice where this happens in their own life, and offers Scripture and small steps toward a quieter mind.

The self-check moves through five areas: the past, the future, decisions, other people and conscience. In the first part, the person meets twenty everyday situations from home, work, parish and community life and chooses what they would usually do. The answers treat preparing well, praying and asking one trusted person for advice as healthy. What the tool looks for is the response that goes beyond what the situation needs: going over things again and again, seeking reassurance or checking repeatedly, or avoiding the matter altogether. The options appear in a different order on each visit, so the healthy answer is never in a fixed place.

The second part turns inward. Twenty short statements ask how the mind has been over the past month, and four more ask what overthinking has been costing in sleep, rest, presence and energy. Three simple yes or no statements gently check for answers that claim a mind that is never troubled.

The result brings the two parts together. For each area the person sees one mark for daily life and one for the mind, an area to give thanks for, and an area where the Lord may be inviting them to grow, with a passage from Scripture, a question for prayer and small steps for the coming weeks. They also learn how their overthinking usually shows itself, whether by replaying, seeking certainty or stepping back. When the cost is high, a note of care encourages them to speak with someone they trust, or with a counsellor or doctor. The self-check closes with a short personal intention.

The tool is a mirror for reflection, not a validated psychological test or a diagnosis, and its results should not be used to compare or evaluate people.

The whole tool is one file, `index.html`, with its watercolour illustrations built in. It runs in any phone or computer browser, even offline. Answers stay on the person's own device and are never sent anywhere, and a "Clear my answers" button removes everything.

## About the developer

**Fr. Solomon Anthony Nirmalan, CMF**, is a Claretian Missionary priest with 15 years of ministry and a behavioural researcher. He is currently pursuing a Master of Arts in Behavioural Sciences, Track in Organizational and Social Systems Development, at De La Salle University. He holds bachelor's degrees in Philosophy and Theology from the National Seminary of Sri Lanka, affiliated with the Pontifical Urbaniana University in Rome.

Leadership is at the centre of his work. He studies how leaders shape organizations and communities, and his thesis focuses on transformative leadership. His research is guided by theoretical pluralism: drawing on several behavioural frameworks together to understand how people make decisions and adapt. He works in applied behavioural science, organizational and leadership dynamics, and environmental psychology, using mixed methods to turn theoretical models into practical tools with real-world impact. His wider interests include community development, socio-economic welfare, social and institutional change, and social justice for marginalized communities. He is fluent in Tamil, Sinhala and English.
